The carrier has to fit under the seat, or your dog doesn’t fly. Almost every mistake people make buying a flight carrier is a fit mistake — the carrier fits the dog but not the seat, or it fits one airline’s seat but not the airline they actually booked. The gate agent finds out for you at the worst possible moment.

So this ranking starts from the number that decides it — the under-seat opening — and works back to the carriers that clear it.

The trap is **height**. A carrier that’s 11 inches tall sails under a United or Delta seat and gets turned away at Southwest or JetBlue, where the opening is only about 8.5 inches high. Before you buy any carrier, check your airline’s actual limit in our dimensions table below, and measure your dog with the sizing guide linked at the bottom. The soft carriers listed have a squish advantage a hard case doesn’t — but only within reason.

## How we scored

Each carrier was measured against five named metrics:

- **Fit under seat (0–10):** Rigid base measured against published under-seat dimensions for United, Delta, and Southwest. Flex tested by pressing the carrier against a 15-degree incline.
- **Ventilation (0–10):** Mesh panel coverage measured as percentage of interior surface area. Infrared temperature reading taken after 20 minutes of simulated use.
- **Build quality (0–10):** Zipper type (metal vs plastic), stitch density, frame rigidity, buckle strength. Stress-tested to 20 lb pull force.
- **Dog comfort (0–10):** Interior dimensions compared to Peanut’s 13-inch back and 8 lb weight. Standing, turning, and lying-down clearance assessed.
- **Value (0–10):** Price relative to the feature set. A $32 carrier that does 80% of the job scores higher than a $70 carrier that does 90%.

Scores are averages of these five metrics. They are judgments based on measurement, not manufacturer claims.
