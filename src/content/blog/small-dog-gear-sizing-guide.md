Sizing small-dog gear wrong is how you end up with a harness that slips off in the parking lot. This is the measurement routine we use for every product on this site.

## The three numbers that matter

**Back length** — from the base of the neck to the base of the tail. This is the single most important number for backpacks and most harnesses.

**Chest girth** — around the widest part of the ribcage, right behind the front legs. Most escape problems come from a chest that’s too small.

**Neck girth** — around the base of the neck, where a collar sits. For harnesses, this matters less, but it’s still worth writing down.

## How to measure

Stand your dog on a flat surface. Use a soft measuring tape.

- Back length: base of neck to base of tail, in a straight line
- Chest: around the ribcage behind the front legs, snug but not tight
- Neck: around the base of the neck

Have someone hold a treat to keep the dog still. If you’re alone, measure while the dog naps — you won’t get a better reading off a moving dog.

## The size chart we use

| Back length | Suggested size |
| --- | --- |
| 6–8 in | XS |
| 9–11 in | S |
| 12–14 in | M |
| 15–17 in | L |
| 18+ in | XL (rare for small dogs) |

Puppies outgrow sizes fast. If the dog is still growing, buy for the chest now, not the back length later — a slightly roomy harness is safer than one that pinches.

## A note on weight

Weight charts on product pages are rough. A long-backed 8 lb corgi needs a different size than a stocky 8 lb French bulldog. When a product page shows both, trust the back-length column.
