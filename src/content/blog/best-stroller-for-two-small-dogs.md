Two small dogs in one stroller sounds practical until you try it. Most “double” strollers are really just narrow compartments stacked side by side — each one too small for a dog to turn around in, and together they create a top-heavy platform that wobbles on anything but perfect pavement.

We tested ten strollers advertised for two small dogs. Peanut (8 lb corgi) rode in nine of them; his friend Luna (6 lb chihuahua) joined him in the five that seemed promising. We measured interior width, wheelbase stability, and whether both dogs could lie down simultaneously without crowding.

Here’s what survived the test.

## How we scored

Each stroller was measured against four named metrics:

- **Interior width (0–10):** Measured the flat floor space inside each compartment. Dogs need to lie down side by side, not stack. Minimum comfortable width for two 8 lb dogs: 20 inches.
- **Wheelbase stability (0–10):** Pushed each stroller over cobblestones with both dogs inside. Recorded wobble severity on a 1–5 scale, doubled. Narrow wheelbase = high tip risk.
- **Fold convenience (0–10):** Timed the fold from standing position, one hand. Scored based on seconds and ease of latching.
- **Value (0–10):** Price relative to the feature set. A $65 stroller that does 80% of the job scores higher than a $120 stroller that does 90%.

Scores are averages of these four metrics. They are judgments based on measurement, not manufacturer claims.

## Two-dog stroller buying rules

If you’re considering a dual-dog stroller, these rules saved us from three bad purchases:

- **Combined weight ≤ 60% of rated limit.** A 30 lb rated stroller should carry no more than 18 lb total. The extra margin accounts for dynamic weight shifts when dogs move.
- **Interior width ≥ 20 inches for two medium dogs.** Anything narrower forces dogs to stack or face each other, which increases anxiety and heat buildup.
- **Wheelbase ≥ 15 inches between rear wheels.** Narrow wheelbases tip on uneven ground. Measure the distance between the outer edges of the rear wheels, not the frame.
- **Divider must be removable.** Fixed dividers create two small cages. A removable divider gives you the option to use it solo later.
- **Test the brake with weight.** A brake that holds an empty stroller on a slope may slide with 14 lb of dog in it. Always test loaded.
