# How we test

<div class="lede">Every review on this site is based on a specific, repeatable test on our own dog, Peanut. Here's the exact method for each one, so you can read any number below with full context — and judge for yourself whether the test means what we say it means.</div>

<div class="rule"></div>

## Pull-force test (harnesses)

We clip a luggage scale between the leash and the harness, then walk the same 20-minute route — past the neighbor's cat, across a road, and up the hill where Peanut loses his mind. We record the peak pull force on each walk and average it over three walks per harness.

- Same route, same time of day, same handler
- Three walks per harness, peak value averaged
- We also note choke risk by watching the strap position at full pull

## Escape test (backpacks, harnesses, slings)

We zip or buckle a dog in exactly as the brand instructs, then have a stranger walk past holding a treat. We count whether the dog can get out within one minute, and whether it takes the stranger to trigger it. Escape is scored on both the dog's effort and the closure's resistance.

## Vibration test (strollers)

We tape a phone running an accelerometer app to the stroller floor, then push over the same 200-meter cobblestone stretch. We compare peak vibration between models, and separately track how many jerks the dog experiences — small dogs with sensitive spines care about that second number more.

## Leak test (water bottles)

Every bottle rides for a week in a backpack pocket — right side up and upside down — and we check for dampness each evening. Then we test the one-hand dispense: can we open it, dispense, and close it while holding the leash in the other hand?

## Fold and durability test (strollers)

We fold and unfold each stroller 40 times, standing up, one-handed where the brand claims it. We check the folding joint for looseness after fold 20 and fold 40. A stroller that needs two hands isn't a failure — but a fold that gets wobbly is.

## What we don't do

We don't crash-test anything. We're not a lab, we're a household with one opinionated corgi, and nothing on this site is professional training or veterinary advice. Where a claim (like crash-test certification) matters and we can't do it ourselves, we say so and point to the official result instead of faking it.

*Test dog: Peanut, an 8 lb corgi. 13-inch back length, a puller, opinionated about transportation.*
